# V2.2 Technical Handoff

## Research question

The project studies whether hourly station-level bike-sharing demand can transfer across European cities, and whether transfer mechanisms improve performance when a target city has little or no labeled adaptation data.

## Cohorts and temporal design

The development cohort contains eight cities: Dortmund (129), Heidelberg (194), Marburg (438), Giessen (467), Cardiff (476), Bilbao (532), Freiburg (619), and Goeteborg (658). Development selection uses four pseudo-target folds: Cardiff, Bilbao, Freiburg, and Goeteborg. Each pseudo-target is excluded from its fold's source cities.

The final held-out cities are Mannheim (195), Innsbruck (199), Glasgow (237), and Split (617). Their evaluation labels remain sealed. The protocol uses fixed UTC temporal boundaries, causal predictor origins, sealed fit snapshots, a target-adaptation window, an embargo/no-update interval, and a later final evaluation interval. Predictions must be committed before the corresponding evaluation labels are opened.

The study is an idealized count-feed benchmark: it assumes station departure counts are available at the registered causal time, while status evidence is constrained by its timestamp. This is an explicit limitation for real-time deployment claims.

## V2.2 causal observability

V2.2 separates causal predictors from retrospective evaluation adjudication. Predictor features and masks at forecast origin `t` may use only information available by `t`; later evidence may determine whether a historical target is evaluable but may not revise earlier predictors or fitted artifacts. A fixed 799-station cohort and node order are bound by hashes. Missing/unobserved demand is distinct from a verified zero.

## Models and frozen selections

The Graph-GRU combines a recurrent demand model with a fixed geographic station graph. The Vanilla-GRU is the non-graph recurrent comparison. Stage 1 froze `LOG1P` target-space MAE. Stage 2 selected `GGRU_K04_H032_D00` (`k=4`, hidden size 32, dropout 0). Stage 2B independently selected `VGRU_H032_D00` (hidden size 32, dropout 0).

In development zero-shot transfer, the Vanilla-GRU slightly outperformed the Graph-GRU. This is a frozen upstream empirical observation, not a final-target result.

Stage 3 preserves full-network AdamW adaptation with a fresh optimizer, learning rate `2e-4`, weight decay `1e-4`, batch size 16, global gradient clipping 1.0, no early stopping, and budgets `[0, 100, 300, 600, 1200]`; budget 0 is checkpoint reuse, not a fit.

Stage 4 evaluates GRL-based multi-source source-domain-invariant pretraining on the frozen Graph-GRU backbone. It is a negative-transfer mitigation mechanism/ablation, not an architecture-novelty claim. The grid is frozen to lambda maxima `[0.01, 0.1, 0.5]`, schedules `[constant, linear]`, seeds `[17, 29, 43]`, and the four pseudo-target folds. Selection uses post-7-day, 300-update adaptation count-space MAE under the prospectively sealed aggregation/tie rule.
